- Another of the cult sci-fi books by Asimov

> *The premise of the stories is that in the waning days of a future* [*Galactic Empire*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Empire_(Isaac_Asimov))*, the mathematician* [*Hari Seldon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Seldon) *devises the theory of* [*psychohistory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychohistory_(fictional))*, a new and effective* [*mathematics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics) *of sociology. Using statistical laws of* [*mass action*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_action_(sociology))*, it can predict the future of large populations. Seldon foresees the imminent fall of the Empire, which encompasses the entire* [*Milky Way*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way)*, and a* [*dark age*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Ages_(historiography)) *lasting 30,000 years before a second empire arises. Although the momentum of the Empire’s fall is too great to stop, Seldon devises a plan by which “the onrushing mass of events must be deflected just a little” to eventually limit this* [*interregnum*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interregnum) *to just one thousand years. The books describe some of the dramatic events of those years as they are shaped by the underlying political and social mechanics of Seldon’s Plan*
